What vitamins are in spinach, and how does cooking affect them?

Spinach (Spinacia oleracea) is an exceptionally nutrient-dense leafy green rich in several essential fat-soluble and water-soluble vitamins:

>400% DV per 100g raw

Critical for blood coagulation cascade and osteocalcin carboxylation for bone mineralization.

Crucial methyl donor for DNA synthesis, methylation repair, and erythropoiesis.

add_circle Also supplies bioavailable Vitamin E (alpha-tocopherol), Vitamin B6, and non-heme Iron (optimized via Vitamin C synergy).
skillet Metabolic Impact of Culinary Heat Application

Light steaming (2–4 minutes) is optimal: it breaks down thick plant cell matrices to enhance bioavailability of fat-soluble beta-carotene and lutein by up to 300%, while significantly degrading anti-nutrient oxalates that bind calcium. Avoid prolonged water boiling, which leaches up to 50–65% of water-soluble ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) into discarded cooking fluid.
